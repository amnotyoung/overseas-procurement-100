## Nepal

### 01. [HIGH] Documents required from a foreign bidder's local agent

- Institution: Nepal — Bidding system
- Legal basis: Public Procurement Rules, 2064 (2007) r.39(1)(d)
- KOICA guide: p.11-13

**The guide says** — The KOICA guide states that a Nepali (local) agent must submit a resident registration document and a power of attorney.

**The law says** — Public Procurement Rules 2064 (2007) r.39(1)(d) requires the attested copy of the agent's Permanent Account Number (PAN) Registration Certificate and a letter of acceptance to become an agent.

**Why it matters** — PAN is a tax registration number, entirely different from a resident registration. Preparing per the guide omits a mandatory document and the bid may be excluded from evaluation. The 'power of attorney' is in fact a 'letter of acceptance' — a different document.

**✔ Verify** — Check the procuring entity's standard bidding documents against PPR r.39(1)(d): confirm a local agent must provide a PAN registration certificate and a letter of acceptance, not a resident registration document.

### 02. [HIGH] Where ODA project formation actually starts

- Institution: Nepal — ODA project pipeline
- Legal basis: Foreign Aid Mobilization Policy, 2025 §3.3.2(k), §3.3.5(b)
- KOICA guide: p.8

**The guide says** — The guide says grant projects are found through consultation between the donor agency and the line ministry.

**The law says** — Foreign Aid Mobilization Policy 2025 §3.3.2(k) requires government agencies to obtain prior Ministry of Finance approval before seeking aid, making proposals, negotiating, sharing information, corresponding on concept papers, or signing MoUs; §3.3.5(b) requires development partners to obtain Ministry approval before mobilizing any aid.

**Why it matters** — Starting with the line ministry skips the prerequisite: that ministry cannot share information or consult without prior MoF approval. Early contact may be flagged as a policy breach or delayed while approval is obtained.

**✔ Verify** — Confirm the MoF (International Economic Relations Division) approval route before contacting a line ministry, and check the recipient agency has prior MoF approval. This applies to the donor agency itself too (§3.3.5(b)).

### 03. [HIGH] Whether the guide's underlying aid policy is still in force

- Institution: Nepal — ODA project pipeline
- Legal basis: Foreign Aid Mobilization Policy, 2025 §3.7.1, §3.7.2 (승인 2025-04-21)
- KOICA guide: p.8

**The guide says** — The guide describes Nepal's aid-receiving system on the basis of the International Development Cooperation Policy 2019.

**The law says** — Foreign Aid Mobilization Policy 2025 §3.7.1 repealed the 2019 policy; the new policy was approved on 21 April 2025.

**Why it matters** — The 2019 policy was valid at the guide's publication (25 Feb 2025) but has since been replaced. The USD 10 million concessional-loan threshold and the National Planning Commission consent requirement are gone, and tax exemption has narrowed. Applying the guide as-is prepares for requirements that no longer exist.

**✔ Verify** — For projects after April 2025, use the Foreign Aid Mobilization Policy 2025, not the 2019 policy. Recheck loan thresholds and tax-exemption assumptions against the new policy.

## Tanzania

### 04. [HIGH] Whether the guide's procurement law is still in force

- Institution: Tanzania — Bidding system
- Legal basis: Public Procurement Act, No. 10 of 2023 §131(1); Public Procurement Regulations, 2025 (GN No. 261)
- KOICA guide: p.213

**The guide says** — The guide bases Tanzania's bidding rules on the Public Procurement Regulations 2013 (and the 2011 Act).

**The law says** — Public Procurement Act No. 10 of 2023 §131(1) repealed the 2011 Act; the new Act took effect on 17 June 2024 (GN 455), and the regulations were replaced by the Public Procurement Regulations 2025 (GN No. 261).

**Why it matters** — The new Act and regulations took effect about eight months before the guide was published (25 Feb 2025) — the guide already cited repealed law. Following it applies the old fixed surety rates and misses the new force-account threshold, price cap, mandatory NeST e-procurement, and stronger local-supplier preference.

**✔ Verify** — For tenders after June 2024, apply PPA 2023 and PPR 2025. Confirm surety amounts in each tender's documents, and check NeST registration and local-preference requirements before bidding.

### 05. [HIGH] Legal basis for PPRA's mandate

- Institution: Tanzania — Procurement governance
- Legal basis: Public Procurement Act, No. 10 of 2023 §8, §74, §131(1)
- KOICA guide: p.210

**The guide says** — The guide describes PPRA's structure and functions on the basis of the 2011 Act framework.

**The law says** — PPA 2023 §8 keeps PPRA in existence but under the new Act; it adds the power to set price caps (§74) and mandatory NeST e-procurement. The 2011 Act was repealed by §131(1).

**Why it matters** — PPRA as an institution continues, but its governing framework changed. Using the guide as-is misses the new price cap, mandatory NeST, and stronger local-supplier preference, and points to section numbers from a repealed Act.

**✔ Verify** — Use PPA 2023 as the reference. Check whether the estimated price is subject to a PPRA price cap, and whether the procuring entity is registered/operating on NeST.

## Nepal

### 06. [MEDIUM] Legal source cited for blacklisting an agent

- Institution: Nepal — Bidding system
- Legal basis: Public Procurement Rules, 2064 (2007) r.39(3), r.141 / Public Procurement Act, 2063 (2007) §63
- KOICA guide: p.11-13

**The guide says** — The guide says a bidder is blacklisted 'under provision 141', without naming the instrument.

**The law says** — Rule 141 belongs to the Public Procurement Rules 2064 (2007), not the Act; the substantive basis is Section 63 of the Public Procurement Act 2063 (2007). The Act has only 76 sections, so no Section 141 exists.

**Why it matters** — A reader looking for Section 141 of the Act cannot find it and loses the trail. The guide's substance is correct — only the source label is incomplete.

**✔ Verify** — No change to bid preparation. When citing the basis, use PPR r.141 (procedure) together with PPA §63 (substance).

### 07. [MEDIUM] Who prepares the Project Concept Paper (PCP)

- Institution: Nepal — ODA project pipeline
- Legal basis: Foreign Aid Mobilization Policy, 2025 §3.3.2(a), §3.3.2(b), §3.3.1(a)
- KOICA guide: p.8

**The guide says** — Two parts of the guide conflict: section II-1 says the line ministry prepares the PCP and sends it via a MoF letter; section III says the MoF itself prepares the PCP.

**The law says** — FAMP 2025 §3.3.2(a)(b) has line ministries prepare a project list and submit proposals to the Ministry per a project readiness checklist; the Ministry receives, reviews and forwards. The preparer is the line ministry.

**Why it matters** — It changes whether the counterpart for PCP consultation is the MoF or the line ministry. Approaching the wrong party at the formation stage wastes time.

**✔ Verify** — Treat the line ministry as the PCP preparer; forwarding goes through a MoF letter. Confirm the readiness-checklist form and submission route with the KOICA Nepal office.

### 08. [MEDIUM] Scope of tax and customs exemption

- Institution: Nepal — ODA project pipeline
- Legal basis: Foreign Aid Mobilization Policy, 2025 §3.6.1(a)(b)(c)(d)
- KOICA guide: p.17

**The guide says** — The guide explains only the master-list procedure for customs exemption (about 3 months), not any limit on the exemption itself.

**The law says** — FAMP 2025 §3.6.1: (a) grants shall generally not be used for tax payments; (b) loan-funded projects shall generally not include tax-exemption provisions; (c) no exemption for importing goods sufficiently produced within Nepal.

**Why it matters** — Under the new policy a master list may not yield exemption for locally-produced goods, and loan-linked projects may have no exemption clause at all. The equipment tax burden may fall outside the budget assumption.

**✔ Verify** — When listing equipment, check whether items are sufficiently produced in Nepal (no exemption). For loan-linked projects, check the financing agreement for any exemption clause.

### 09. [MEDIUM] Who publishes and handles tenders — PPMO vs procuring entities

- Institution: Nepal — Procurement governance
- Legal basis: Public Procurement Act, 2063 (2007) §65(1)(f), §65(2)
- KOICA guide: p.9

**The guide says** — The guide says PPMO 'oversees' the publication of tenders by industry sector.

**The law says** — PPA 2063 (2007) §65(1)(f) gives PPMO the power to set up and operate the procurement website; §65(2) bars PPMO from involving in any manner in other entities' procurement proceedings or resolving disputes. Individual tenders are published and contracted by each procuring entity.

**Why it matters** — Reading 'PPMO oversees tenders' leads a bidder to miss the actual procuring entity and to file complaints with PPMO, which has no power to handle them — wasting the 7-day complaint window.

**✔ Verify** — Confirm each tender's issuing and contracting body is the individual procuring entity, not PPMO. Complaints go to the head of the procuring entity (§47), then the Review Committee (§49).

## Tanzania

### 10. [MEDIUM] Mandatory e-procurement (NeST)

- Institution: Tanzania — Bidding system
- Legal basis: Public Procurement Act, No. 10 of 2023 (전자조달 규정) — PPRA 시행 공지
- KOICA guide: p.214

**The guide says** — The guide describes the bidding process but does not mention mandatory e-procurement.

**The law says** — PPA 2023 mandates the National e-Procurement System of Tanzania (NeST) for all procurement, replacing the former TANePS.

**Why it matters** — Without NeST registration and e-bidding capability, a firm cannot participate at all. Preparing for a paper-based process makes bidding impossible.

**✔ Verify** — Confirm NeST registration is complete before any tender, and that the procuring entity runs the tender through NeST.

### 11. [MEDIUM] Surety rates (bid / performance / retention) and liquidated damages

- Institution: Tanzania — Bidding system
- Legal basis: Public Procurement Regulations, 2025 (GN No. 261) reg.23(6), reg.29(2), reg.315(2), reg.318(2), reg.329(2)(7), Third Schedule
- KOICA guide: p.213

**The guide says** — The guide gives fixed rates: bid security 5%, performance security 10%, defect security 3%, defects-liability period 1-2 years, and liquidated damages of 0.15% (manufacturing) and 0.5% (transport/storage).

**The law says** — PPR 2025 delegates the amounts rather than fixing them: bid security (reg.23(6)) and performance security (reg.29(2)) are set in the tender documents; the defect surety is retention money set by the contract (reg.329(2)); the defects-liability period is 'the period specified in the contract' (reg.329(7)). The only fixed values are the advance-payment cap of 30% (reg.315(2)) and liquidated damages of 0.10-0.20% (goods), 0.10-0.15% (works), 0.10-0.20% (consultancy) per day (reg.318(2)). There is no 'transport/storage' liquidated-damages category.

**Why it matters** — The guide's fixed rates come from the repealed 2013 regulations. Budgeting on them may not match the actual tender/contract terms. The 0.5% transport/storage rate has no basis in the new regulations.

**✔ Verify** — Read the bid and performance security amounts from each tender's documents; read retention money and the defects-liability period from the contract. Do not assume 5% / 10% / 3% / 1-2 years. Check whether any 'transport/storage' liquidated damages exist as a contract-specific special term.

### 12. [MEDIUM] Whether Ministry of Finance approval is a prerequisite for project formation

- Institution: Tanzania — ODA project pipeline
- Legal basis: 자료집 Ⅱ-1 수원체계 (탄자니아 재정기획부 원조사업요청 승인)
- KOICA guide: p.208-209

**The guide says** — The guide says line ministries and local governments may identify projects directly, with MoF final approval added as a proviso.

**The law says** — Per the guide's own text, direct identification is possible only where the Ministry of Finance grants final approval of the aid-project request; the External Finance Department is the single gateway for aid mobilization.

**Why it matters** — Reading 'direct identification possible' and advancing with a line ministry alone can stall formalization (RD signing) while MoF approval is obtained. Approval is a prerequisite, not an option.

**✔ Verify** — Confirm the MoF External Finance Department approval route at the outset. A Record of Discussion (RD) cannot be signed without it.

## How to use this sheet

1. Work through the HIGH items first — those are where following the guide can cause real loss.
2. For each item, do the **Verify** action: open the cited article in the original law
   (links are in each institution's *Legal source* section) or ask the procuring entity.
3. Record the outcome. If the guide is wrong, the correction is already drafted in Korean in
   `docs/errata.md` for transmission to the guide's publisher (KOICA headquarters).

This sheet is a verification aid, not an official interpretation. The original-language law
prevails where a translation differs.
